# Biorepository Manual Knowledge Base

## Big Picture (2-Minute Summary)

**Purpose**

The AlcHepNet Biorepository & Laboratory Manual provides standardized operating procedures for the collection, processing, labeling, storage, shipment, receipt, inventory management, quality control, and distribution of biospecimens collected through the Alcoholic Hepatitis Clinical and Translational Network (AlcHepNet).

Unlike the clinical study protocols, this manual focuses on laboratory operations and specimen management rather than participant eligibility, treatment, or clinical outcomes.

---

**Supported Studies**

The biorepository supports specimen collection for:

- Late Phase Clinical Trials
- Observational Studies
- Translational Research Studies

---

**Primary Goal**

To ensure that biological specimens collected across all participating AlcHepNet clinical sites are processed identically so that downstream laboratory analyses are reliable, reproducible, and comparable across sites and over time.

The manual standardizes:

- laboratory supplies
- specimen collection
- processing workflows
- aliquoting
- labeling
- storage
- shipment
- inventory management
- specimen release
- quality assurance
- biosafety procedures

# 4. Specimen Processing

## General Processing Principles

All biospecimens collected as part of AlcHepNet studies must be processed according to standardized procedures to minimize pre-analytical variability between participating sites. Laboratory personnel should be trained in the procedures before processing study specimens.

General processing principles include:

- Process specimens as soon as possible after collection.
- Follow specimen-specific processing protocols.
- Use protocol-approved collection tubes, cryovials, and laboratory supplies (or approved equivalents).
- Label all aliquots with UMMS-provided barcode labels before freezing.
- Record all required processing information in the biospecimen tracking documentation.
- Maintain specimen identity and chain of custody throughout processing.
- Minimize unnecessary freeze–thaw cycles.
- Follow biosafety precautions for handling human specimens.
- Document any deviations from protocol-defined procedures.

Whenever possible, processing should occur immediately after specimen collection to maximize specimen quality for future biomarker, genomic, proteomic, metabolomic, and translational analyses.

---

## Processing Workflow

Although each specimen type has unique procedures, the general workflow is consistent across the manual.

### Step 1 – Specimen Receipt

- Verify participant study ID.
- Verify visit/time point.
- Confirm specimen type.
- Inspect collection tubes for integrity.
- Inspect specimens for hemolysis, clotting, leakage, or other abnormalities.
- Verify specimen labels before beginning processing.

---

### Step 2 – Processing Preparation

Prepare all required materials before opening collection tubes.

Typical preparation includes:

- Cryovials
- Barcode labels
- Pipettes
- Serological pipettes
- Tube racks
- Ice (when required)
- PBS or other processing reagents
- Centrifuge
- Biological safety cabinet when required

---

### Step 3 – Centrifugation

Multiple specimen types require centrifugation.

The protocol specifies specimen-specific:

- centrifugal force (× g)
- duration
- temperature
- rotor type when applicable
- brake settings when applicable

Examples include:

- Platelet-poor plasma
- Platelet-rich plasma
- Serum
- HCl acidified plasma
- CPT tubes

The MOP emphasizes using identical centrifugation procedures across all participating sites to minimize laboratory variability.

---

### Step 4 – Separation

Following centrifugation, laboratory staff separate the required biological components.

Examples include:

- plasma
- serum
- PBMC layer
- buffy coat
- whole blood aliquots
- red blood cell fractions

Care should be taken to avoid disturbing adjacent cellular layers during aspiration.

---

### Step 5 – Aliquot Preparation

Each specimen is divided into protocol-defined aliquots.

Reasons for standardized aliquoting include:

- reducing freeze–thaw cycles
- preserving material for multiple future studies
- maintaining consistency across all participating sites
- facilitating specimen sharing among investigators

Aliquot number and volume depend on specimen type.

---

### Step 6 – Barcode Labeling

Every aliquot receives a standardized UMMS barcode label.

Barcode labels identify:

- participant ID
- specimen type
- aliquot designation
- visit
- barcode identifier

Color-coded cap inserts are also standardized for rapid visual identification of specimen type.

---

### Step 7 – Freezing

Processed specimens should be frozen immediately according to specimen-specific storage requirements.

Most biospecimens are stored at:

- −80°C

PBMC specimens undergo controlled-rate freezing before long-term storage.

---

### Step 8 – Documentation

Processing information is recorded using AlcHepNet biospecimen documentation procedures.

Documentation includes:

- collection date/time
- processing date/time
- processor initials
- aliquot numbers
- deviations
- freezer location
- shipment status

---

## Centrifugation Requirements

The MOP specifies specimen-specific centrifugation procedures.

Important parameters include:

| Parameter | Importance |
|-----------|------------|
| Relative centrifugal force (× g) | Standardizes plasma and serum preparation |
| Duration | Ensures complete separation |
| Temperature | Maintains specimen integrity |
| Rotor type | Standardizes processing across sites |
| Brake setting | Prevents disturbance of cellular layers |

The manual standardizes these parameters so biospecimens collected at different institutions remain directly comparable.

---

## Aliquot Preparation

Aliquot preparation follows standardized procedures.

General principles include:

- Use sterile cryovials.
- Avoid cross-contamination.
- Prepare aliquots immediately after processing.
- Use the aliquot numbers specified for each specimen type.
- Cap tubes securely.
- Inspect aliquots before freezing.

Benefits of standardized aliquoting include:

- long-term preservation,
- efficient specimen retrieval,
- reduced freeze–thaw exposure,
- support for multiple future translational studies.

---

## Labeling Requirements

The AlcHepNet biorepository uses standardized barcode labels supplied by UMMS.

Labels should be:

- attached before freezing,
- securely adhered,
- placed consistently on cryovials,
- protected from moisture and frost.

Color-coded cap inserts provide rapid identification of specimen type during storage and retrieval.

Barcode labels are also used for:

- shipment verification,
- inventory management,
- specimen retrieval,
- research distribution.

Requests for additional barcode labels should be submitted approximately **2 weeks before they are needed**.

---

## Processing Time Requirements

The MOP emphasizes minimizing delays between collection and processing.

General recommendations include:

- Begin processing immediately whenever possible.
- Avoid prolonged room-temperature storage.
- Follow specimen-specific timing requirements.
- Freeze processed specimens promptly.
- Transport temperature-sensitive specimens appropriately before processing.

For stool specimens specifically, transport on ice is preferred. If immediate transport on ice is not possible, room-temperature exposure should be limited to approximately **30 minutes** before freezing. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

## Documentation

All specimen processing activities must be documented.

Required documentation includes:

- participant identifier
- specimen type
- visit number
- collection time
- processing time
- aliquot information
- storage location
- processor initials
- protocol deviations

The MOP includes REDCap Biospecimen Tracking and Processing Forms to standardize documentation across participating sites. Documentation procedures were expanded in later revisions of the manual to improve specimen traceability. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

## Processing Deviations

Any deviation from protocol-defined processing procedures should be documented.

Examples include:

- delayed processing
- insufficient specimen volume
- labeling errors
- equipment malfunction
- temperature excursions
- centrifugation failure
- damaged collection tubes
- specimen clotting
- hemolysis
- shipping delays affecting processing

Documentation should include:

- description of the deviation,
- corrective action,
- assessment of specimen usability,
- investigator or laboratory notification when appropriate.

Maintaining complete documentation of deviations helps preserve specimen quality and supports downstream interpretation of laboratory analyses.

# 5. Specimen Labeling Guide

## Purpose

The AlcHepNet Biorepository uses a standardized labeling system to uniquely identify every biospecimen collected across participating clinical sites while protecting participant confidentiality. Labels are generated centrally by the UMMS Biorepository and supplied to each participating site before specimen collection. Standardized labeling supports accurate specimen identification throughout collection, processing, storage, shipment, inventory management, and future research use. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

## Label Distribution

All cryovial labels are:

- Printed by the UMMS Biorepository.
- Distributed to participating study sites before enrollment.
- Prepared for all anticipated participant visits.
- Supplied for both observational studies and randomized clinical trials.

When a site has approximately **five label sheets remaining**, additional labels should be requested. Requests should be submitted with **at least two weeks' notice** to allow sufficient time for processing and shipment. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

> **Important:** The Data Coordinating Center provides labels only for research cryovials shipped to the biorepository. Clinical blood collection tubes should continue to be labeled according to each site's local laboratory procedures. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

## Label Structure

Each cryovial label contains a standardized **13-character identifier** that uniquely identifies the biospecimen.

The identifier consists of five components:

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Subject ID | Five-digit participant study identifier, including site and subsite information |
| Study Identifier | Indicates whether the specimen originated from the randomized clinical trial or observational study |
| Study Day / Week | Indicates the scheduled collection time point |
| Specimen Type | Two-character abbreviation identifying the biospecimen |
| Aliquot Number | Sequential aliquot number for that specimen |

Example:

```
99009R007SE01
```

This example represents:

- Subject ID: **99009**
- Study: **R** (Randomized Clinical Trial)
- Collection Day: **007 (Day 7)**
- Specimen Type: **SE (Serum)**
- Aliquot Number: **01** :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

## Label Application

Proper label placement is essential for maintaining readability during long-term frozen storage.

Laboratory personnel should:

- Apply labels parallel to the length of the cryovial.
- Never place labels on the cap.
- Ensure that the cap can be opened without damaging the label.
- Secure each label with clear tape before freezing.
- Verify specimen identity before storage. :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

# 6.3 Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cell (PBMC) Collection and Preparation

## Purpose

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) are isolated from whole blood to preserve viable immune cells for future translational, immunologic, genomic, and mechanistic studies. Because PBMC viability is highly sensitive to collection and processing conditions, the MOP specifies a tightly standardized workflow that must be followed at every participating site. 

---

## Collection Schedule

PBMCs are collected according to the protocol-defined specimen schedule using:

- **Three 8 mL CPT sodium citrate tubes**
- Total blood volume approximately **24 mL**
- Quarterly shipment after processing and storage at the local site. 

---

## Materials Required

PBMC processing requires:

- 8 mL BD Vacutainer CPT Mononuclear Cell Preparation Tubes (Na citrate)
- 50 mL conical centrifuge tubes
- 1.8 mL cryovials with **orange caps**
- 2 mL cryovials with **pink caps** for CPT plasma
- Phosphate Buffered Saline (PBS)
- Heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS)
- Cell culture-grade DMSO
- Controlled-rate freezing media (10% DMSO in FBS)
- Controlled-rate freezing container (Mr. Frosty™ or Corning CoolCell™)
- Swing-bucket centrifuge
- Pipettes and sterile serological pipettes
- −80°C freezer (or liquid nitrogen after initial freezing) 

---

## Reagent Preparation

### Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS)

Prior to processing:

- Thaw frozen FBS aliquots.
- Store thawed aliquots at **2–8°C**.
- Mix thoroughly before use.
- Once thawed, FBS remains stable for **up to one month** (or until the manufacturer's expiration date, whichever occurs first).
- Avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles.
- Do not refreeze refrigerated aliquots. 

---

### Phosphate Buffered Saline (PBS)

PBS should:

- be warmed to **room temperature (15–30°C)** before use,
- be used during the washing steps of PBMC isolation,
- remain sterile throughout processing. 

---

### Freezing Medium

PBMC cryopreservation requires standardized freezing media consisting of:

- **1 part DMSO**
- **9 parts FBS**

Preparation requirements:

- Prepare before beginning specimen processing.
- Chill for at least:
  - **30 minutes in a refrigerator (2–8°C)**, or
  - **15 minutes in an ice bath.**

The controlled-rate freezing container should also be preconditioned according to the manufacturer's instructions before use. 

---

## PBMC Isolation Procedure

### Step 1 – Record Collection Time

Immediately document:

- date of blood collection,
- time of blood collection.

Processing timelines are calculated from this collection time. 

---

### Step 2 – Temporary Storage

Following collection:

- keep CPT tubes upright,
- store at **room temperature (15–30°C)**,
- begin processing within **12 hours** of blood collection.

Longer delays may compromise cell viability. 

---

### Step 3 – Mix Blood

Immediately before centrifugation:

- gently invert CPT tubes **8–10 times**
- remix cellular components uniformly.

Record the time centrifugation begins. 

---

### Step 4 – Primary Centrifugation

Centrifuge using:

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Temperature | Room temperature (15–30°C) |
| Rotor | Horizontal swing-out rotor |
| Relative centrifugal force | **1800 × g** |
| Duration | **30 minutes** |
| Brake | **OFF** |

Brake use is specifically prohibited because abrupt stopping may disturb the PBMC separation layer. 

---

### Step 5 – Plasma Separation

Following centrifugation:

- carefully remove the CPT tubes,
- identify the separated plasma layer,
- aspirate plasma without disturbing the mononuclear cell layer.

Aliquot plasma into **2 mL cryovials with pink caps**.

Typical aliquot volume:

- **1.5 mL per cryovial**
- usually **2–3 aliquots per CPT tube**

Freeze CPT plasma immediately at **−80°C**. 

---

### Step 6 – Collect PBMC Layer

Using a sterile pipette:

- collect the mononuclear cell layer,
- transfer cells into sterile 50 mL conical tubes.

If processing multiple CPT tubes:

- combine **no more than three CPT tubes** into one 50 mL tube.
- Additional CPT tubes require separate processing tubes.

This prevents excessive cell density during washing. 

---

### Step 7 – First Wash

For each conical tube:

- add PBS equal to **three times the cell-layer volume**
- maximum total volume **45 mL**
- gently invert five times.

Centrifuge:

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|------:|
| Speed | **300 × g** |
| Time | **10 minutes** |
| Temperature | Room temperature |

Carefully aspirate the supernatant while preserving the cell pellet. 

---

### Step 8 – Second Wash

Resuspend the pellet.

Bring the volume back to **45 mL** using PBS.

Mix gently.

Repeat centrifugation:

- 300 × g
- 10 minutes
- room temperature

Remove the supernatant while avoiding disturbance of the pellet. 

---

### Step 9 – Cell Counting

Resuspend the pellet in **10 mL PBS**.

Determine:

- total cell count,
- live cell count,
- dead cell count.

The **total live cell count** is recorded as the official PBMC yield for the participant. 

---

### Step 10 – Final Wash

Following cell counting:

- bring total volume back to **45 mL** with PBS,
- centrifuge again at **300 × g for 10 minutes**,
- remove the supernatant while preserving the pellet. 

---

### Step 11 – Cryopreservation

Resuspend the final pellet in:

**10 mL chilled freezing medium (10% DMSO in FBS).**

Aliquot into orange-cap cryovials containing approximately:

- **1–2 million viable cells per tube.**

Approximately **11 PBMC aliquots** are expected from each participant. 

---

### Step 12 – Controlled-Rate Freezing

Immediately after aliquoting:

- place cryovials into a controlled-rate freezing container (Mr. Frosty™ or CoolCell™),
- transfer the freezing container to a freezer maintained between **−60°C and −90°C (typically −80°C).**

Record:

- date frozen,
- time placed into freezer. 

---

### Step 13 – Long-Term Storage

After controlled-rate freezing (approximately 24 hours):

PBMC aliquots may be transferred to:

- long-term **−80°C storage**, or
- **liquid nitrogen** storage when available.

Maintaining an uninterrupted cold chain preserves long-term cellular viability for future translational investigations. 

---

## Critical Quality Considerations

The PBMC protocol emphasizes several quality-control measures:

- Complete all processing within **12 hours** of blood collection.
- Never use the centrifuge brake during primary CPT separation.
- Avoid disturbing the PBMC interface during plasma removal.
- Limit each 50 mL tube to material from no more than three CPT tubes.
- Perform all wash steps using standardized centrifugation parameters.
- Record live cell counts before freezing.
- Use controlled-rate freezing for every PBMC specimen.
- Maintain complete documentation of collection, processing, freezing, and storage times. 

---

## Main Takeaway

PBMC processing is the most technically complex biospecimen protocol within the AlcHepNet Biorepository. Strict adherence to standardized centrifugation, washing, cell counting, cryopreservation, and controlled-rate freezing procedures is essential to preserve viable immune cells and ensure specimens collected across all participating clinical sites remain suitable for future mechanistic, immunologic, and translational research.

# 6.4 Platelet-Poor Plasma Collection and Preparation

## Purpose

Platelet-poor plasma (PPP) is collected for future biomarker, cytokine, metabolomic, proteomic, and other translational laboratory analyses. The objective is to obtain plasma with minimal residual platelets, reducing variability in downstream laboratory testing. To achieve this, the MOP specifies a standardized double-centrifugation protocol that must be performed consistently across all participating sites. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

## Collection Schedule

- **Visits:** According to Figure 6.1 study schedule
- **Batch shipment:** Quarterly
- **Shipment coordination:** All participating sites ship during the same designated week coordinated by the UMMS Biorepository. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

## Materials Required

Required materials include:

- Human blood specimen
- Preprinted UMMS barcode labels
- **BD Vacutainer Citrate Tube**
  - 4.5 mL
  - Light blue top
  - Catalog **SWD340478**
  - Verify tubes have not expired
- Disposable serological pipettes
- 2 mL cryovials with **light blue screw caps**
- Swing-bucket bench-top centrifuge
- Rubber bands for grouping aliquots
- −80°C freezer :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

## Important Collection Note

Four citrate tubes are collected during study visits.

However:

- **Only three tubes** are processed for platelet-poor plasma.
- **The fourth tube** is reserved exclusively for **HCl acidified plasma**, which follows a separate protocol (Section 6.8). :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

---

## Platelet-Poor Plasma Processing Procedure

### Step 1 – Blood Collection

Draw **4.5 mL of blood** into each citrate Vacutainer.

Important:

- Completely fill each tube.
- Underfilled tubes may alter the anticoagulant-to-blood ratio and affect plasma quality. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

### Step 2 – Mix Anticoagulant

Immediately after collection:

- gently invert each tube **8–10 times**.

Avoid vigorous shaking.

Samples should either:

- undergo centrifugation immediately, or
- be placed on ice and centrifuged within **2 hours** of collection. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

---

### Step 3 – First Centrifugation

Centrifuge each citrate tube using:

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Speed | **1500 × g** |
| Duration | **15 minutes** |
| Temperature | Room temperature |
| Rotor | Swing-out rotor |

Because centrifuge RPM varies by instrument, laboratories should use **relative centrifugal force (RCF)** rather than RPM when configuring the centrifuge. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

---

### Step 4 – Plasma Separation

After centrifugation, three distinct layers should be visible:

1. Plasma
2. Buffy coat (leukocytes)
3. Erythrocytes

Using a sterile pipette:

- carefully aspirate only the plasma layer,
- transfer plasma into a new plastic tube,
- avoid disturbing the buffy coat,
- avoid transferring any cellular material.

Residual leukocytes or platelets may affect downstream biomarker analyses. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

---

### Step 5 – Second Centrifugation

The collected plasma undergoes a second centrifugation to further reduce platelet contamination.

Centrifuge conditions:

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Speed | **1500 × g** |
| Duration | **15 minutes** |
| Temperature | Room temperature |

Following centrifugation:

- leave approximately **100 μL** of plasma above the pellet,
- avoid aspirating material from the bottom of the tube,
- transfer the remaining platelet-poor plasma into cryovials. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

---

### Step 6 – Aliquoting

Prepare:

- **Six total aliquots**
- **1.0 mL per aliquot**
- 2 mL cryovials
- Light blue screw caps

Apply the appropriate preprinted platelet-poor plasma barcode label to each cryovial before freezing.

Ensure labels follow the standardized labeling instructions described in Section 5. :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

---

### Step 7 – Freezing

Immediately after aliquoting:

- group cryovials together using a rubber band,
- transfer directly to a **−80°C freezer**,
- minimize delay between aliquoting and freezing.

Rapid freezing preserves plasma proteins and other temperature-sensitive analytes for future research. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}

---

## Expected Yield

From approximately **9–13.5 mL** of collected citrate blood (three 4.5 mL tubes), the protocol expects:

- **6 platelet-poor plasma aliquots**
- **1.0 mL per aliquot**
- Stored in **2 mL light blue-cap cryovials**. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

---

## Critical Quality Considerations

The platelet-poor plasma SOP emphasizes several important quality-control measures:

- Completely fill citrate collection tubes.
- Mix anticoagulant by gentle inversion (8–10 inversions).
- Process specimens immediately whenever possible.
- If delayed, keep specimens on ice and process within **2 hours**.
- Carefully avoid disturbing the buffy coat during plasma aspiration.
- Perform both centrifugation steps using identical parameters.
- Leave approximately **100 μL** above the pellet after the second centrifugation.
- Freeze aliquots immediately after preparation.
- Verify barcode labels before storage. :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}

---

## Main Takeaway

Platelet-poor plasma preparation relies on a standardized **two-step centrifugation protocol** designed to remove platelets and cellular contaminants while preserving soluble plasma biomarkers. Strict adherence to collection volume, centrifugation conditions, careful plasma aspiration, standardized aliquoting, and immediate −80°C storage ensures that platelet-poor plasma specimens collected across all AlcHepNet sites remain directly comparable for future translational and biomarker research.

# 6.5 Remaining Specimen-Specific Processing Protocols

The remaining biospecimen protocols follow the same standardized workflow described in previous sections: collection using protocol-approved tubes, specimen-specific centrifugation (when applicable), aliquoting into pre-labeled cryovials, immediate freezing, documentation, and quarterly shipment to the UMMS Biorepository. Differences between protocols primarily involve collection tubes, aliquot volumes, processing requirements, and intended downstream analyses.

---

## Serum

### Purpose

Serum is collected for future biomarker, proteomic, metabolomic, and clinical laboratory studies.

### Collection

- Collect blood into a 6 mL red-top serum tube.
- Allow blood to clot before centrifugation.
- Centrifuge according to protocol-defined conditions.
- Carefully transfer serum without disturbing the clot.

### Aliquoting

- Six aliquots
- Approximately 0.75 mL per aliquot
- Red-cap cryovials
- Freeze immediately at −80°C.

---

## Anakinra Trough Serum

Collected only from participants receiving anakinra in the randomized clinical trial.

Purpose:

- Measurement of trough drug concentrations.
- Pharmacokinetic analyses.

Processing follows the standard serum protocol with separate labeling and storage.

---

## Whole Blood (DNA)

### Purpose

Whole blood is preserved for future genomic and DNA-based analyses.

Processing:

- Collect in green-top tube.
- Aliquot approximately 1.5 mL whole blood.
- Freeze immediately without centrifugation.
- Store at −80°C.

---

## Red Blood Cell Lysate

Following whole-blood processing:

- Remove plasma.
- Prepare lysed RBC fraction according to protocol.
- Prepare three aliquots.
- Freeze immediately.

These specimens support future molecular analyses involving erythrocytes.

---

## HCl Acidified Plasma

Applicable only to specified alcoholic hepatitis and heavy-drinking participants.

Purpose:

- Stabilization of acid-sensitive analytes.

Processing differs from platelet-poor plasma by adding hydrochloric acid immediately after plasma preparation before aliquoting and freezing.

---

## Platelet-Rich Plasma

Purpose:

- Preserve platelet-derived biomarkers.

Processing differs from platelet-poor plasma by using centrifugation conditions that intentionally retain platelets within the plasma fraction.

Typical yield:

- Six aliquots
- Approximately 0.5 mL each
- Lavender-cap cryovials

Store at −80°C.

---

## Urine

### Purpose

Urine supports metabolomic, biomarker, and kidney-related analyses.

Collection:

- Approximately 40 mL.
- Mix thoroughly before aliquoting.
- Seven aliquots prepared.
- Yellow-cap cryovials.
- Freeze immediately at −80°C.

---

## Stool

### Purpose

Stool specimens support microbiome and gastrointestinal research.

Collection:

- Precision™ Stool Collector.
- Transport on ice whenever possible.
- If ice is unavailable, limit room-temperature exposure to approximately 30 minutes.
- Prepare four aliquots.
- Freeze immediately at −80°C.

---

## Saliva

### Purpose

Saliva provides an alternative source of DNA and other molecular biomarkers.

Collection:

- OMNIgene saliva collection kit.
- Follow manufacturer instructions.
- Mix stabilizing solution thoroughly.
- Store according to kit instructions before shipment.

---

## Liver Biopsy Tissue

### Purpose

Liver tissue supports histologic, molecular, and mechanistic studies.

Processing:

- Collect according to study protocol.
- Transfer immediately into designated storage container.
- Freeze rapidly according to protocol.
- Maintain strict chain of custody throughout handling.

---

Across all biospecimen types, the MOP emphasizes:

- Correct collection tube selection.
- Prompt specimen processing.
- Accurate barcode labeling.
- Standardized aliquot volumes.
- Immediate freezing after processing.

- Documentation of any protocol deviations.

# 7. Documentation, Shipping, Storage, and Quality Management

## Documentation

Accurate documentation is essential for maintaining specimen traceability throughout the entire biospecimen lifecycle. Every specimen should be traceable from collection through processing, storage, shipment, and future research use.

Required documentation includes:

- Participant study identifier
- Visit/time point
- Specimen type
- Collection date and time
- Processing date and time
- Processor initials
- Aliquot number(s)
- Storage location
- Shipment date
- Protocol deviations
- Comments when applicable

The AlcHepNet Biorepository uses standardized REDCap Biospecimen Tracking and Processing Forms to maintain consistency across all participating clinical sites.

---

## Shipping Procedures

After processing, biospecimens remain at the local study site until scheduled shipment to the UMMS Biorepository.

### Shipping Frequency

- Quarterly batch shipments
- Coordinated across all participating sites
- Shipment dates provided by the UMMS Biorepository

### Shipping Requirements

Specimens should be shipped:

- Frozen on sufficient dry ice
- Using approved biological specimen shipping containers
- With appropriate absorbent material and secondary containment
- With completed shipping manifests and chain-of-custody documentation

Every shipment should include:

- Specimen inventory
- Shipping manifest
- Tracking information
- Site contact information

Participating sites should notify the Biorepository before shipment and provide tracking information once specimens have been dispatched.

---

## Receipt at the UMMS Biorepository

Upon arrival, repository personnel:

- Inspect each shipment for integrity.
- Verify dry ice quantity and shipping conditions.
- Check specimen labeling and barcode readability.
- Compare received specimens against the shipping manifest.
- Record any discrepancies or shipping issues.
- Enter specimens into the repository inventory system.
- Transfer specimens to long-term storage.

Any damaged, missing, mislabeled, or thawed specimens are documented and communicated to the submitting site.

---

## Long-Term Storage

Following receipt, specimens are maintained under standardized storage conditions designed to preserve long-term specimen integrity.

General storage principles include:

- Maintain continuous ultra-low-temperature storage (typically **−80°C**).
- Monitor freezer temperatures continuously.
- Use alarm systems to detect temperature excursions.
- Maintain backup power and emergency response plans.
- Restrict freezer access to authorized personnel.
- Maintain complete inventory records for every specimen.

PBMC specimens may also be maintained in liquid nitrogen storage when appropriate.

- Rack

- Shipment history
- Distribution history
