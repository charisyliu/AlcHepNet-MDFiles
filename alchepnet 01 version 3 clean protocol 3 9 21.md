# Alcoholic Hepatitis Network Observational Study

## Summary

The **Alcoholic Hepatitis Network (AlcHepNet) Observational Study** is a **prospective, multicenter observational study** established to create a standardized clinical and biospecimen repository for alcoholic hepatitis (AH) research.

The study enrolls three participant groups:

- Patients with alcoholic hepatitis (AH)
- Heavy drinking controls without significant liver disease
- Healthy controls

Clinical information, laboratory data, questionnaire responses, and biological specimens are collected using standardized procedures across all participating sites. Alcoholic hepatitis cases are followed for **24 weeks**, with continued long-term survival follow-up every **24 weeks for up to 5 years** through medical record review and the National Death Index. Heavy drinking controls are followed for **24 weeks**, while healthy controls participate only in the baseline visit.

The primary purpose is to support future investigations into the **epidemiology, diagnosis, pathophysiology, natural history, and treatment of alcoholic hepatitis**, while simultaneously establishing a comprehensive biospecimen repository for translational research.

**Protocol source:** Executive Summary (Section 2, pp. 8–9).

---

# Study Objectives

### Primary Objective

- To collect and store clinical data to facilitate investigations of the epidemiology, diagnosis, pathophysiology, natural history, and treatment of alcoholic hepatitis.

**Protocol source:** Sections 2.1 and 4.2 (pp. 8, 14).

---

### Secondary Objective

- To develop a bio-specimen bank comprised of plasma, serum, peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC), saliva, stool, urine, liver biopsy, DNA, and other biological specimens obtained from patients with alcoholic hepatitis, heavy drinkers without clinical liver disease, and healthy subjects to support translational research in the pathophysiology of alcoholic hepatitis.

**Protocol source:** Sections 2.1 and 4.2 (pp. 8, 14).

---

## Study Design

Describe the overall design and organization of the study.

- **Study type:** Prospective observational study
- **Prospective / retrospective:** Prospective
- **Observational / interventional:** Observational
- **Multicenter:** Yes (8 participating U.S. academic medical centers)
- **Planned enrollment:** Approximately 1,260 participants
  - 720 Alcoholic Hepatitis cases
  - 360 Heavy Drinking Controls
  - 180 Healthy Controls
- **Study duration:** Up to 5 years
- **Major study phases:**
  - **Screening:** Eligibility assessment and informed consent
  - **Baseline:** Clinical assessment, questionnaires, laboratory testing, biospecimen collection
  - **Follow-up:** Scheduled visits according to participant group with long-term survival follow-up for AH cases

Participating clinical sites include:

- Indiana University
- University of Louisville
- Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center
- Mayo Clinic
- Cleveland Clinic
- University of Pittsburgh Medical Center
- University of Texas Southwestern
- Virginia Commonwealth University

The study uses standardized clinical procedures and biospecimen collection across all participating centers to support future observational studies, biomarker research, and clinical trials.

**Protocol source:** Executive Summary (Section 2, pp. 8–9); Research Strategy (Section 4, pp. 13–16).

## Eligibility Criteria

### Alcoholic Hepatitis Cases

**Key Inclusion Criteria**

Participants must:

- Have a clinical diagnosis of alcoholic hepatitis according to the NIAAA Pan-Consortia definition.
- Have onset of jaundice (serum total bilirubin >3 mg/dL) within 8 weeks before screening.
- Have a history of heavy alcohol consumption:
  - Women: >40 g/day or >280 g/week for at least 6 months.
  - Men: >60 g/day or >420 g/week for at least 6 months.
  - Less than 8 weeks of abstinence before onset of jaundice.
- Have AST >50 IU/L.
- Have AST:ALT ratio >1.5 with both values <400 IU/L.
- Have serum total bilirubin >3 mg/dL.
- Be able (or have a legally authorized representative able) to provide written informed consent.
- Be at least 21 years of age.
- Participants may re-enroll up to four times if a new episode of alcoholic hepatitis occurs at least 24 weeks after their previous enrollment.

**Key Exclusion Criteria**

- Liver disease primarily caused by hemochromatosis, autoimmune liver disease, Wilson disease, or acute viral hepatitis.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.
- Prior liver transplantation.
- Investigator determines the participant cannot understand or comply with study requirements.

*Participants with chronic hepatitis B, chronic hepatitis C, HIV infection, or stage 1 hepatocellular carcinoma (one lesion <2 cm) remain eligible according to the protocol.*

**Protocol source:** Section 3, pp. 10–11.

---

### Heavy Drinking Controls

**Key Inclusion Criteria**

Participants must:

- Have chronic heavy alcohol consumption sufficient to cause liver injury:
  - Women: >40 g/day or >280 g/week.
  - Men: >60 g/day or >420 g/week.
- Have maintained this drinking pattern for at least 6 months with less than 8 weeks of abstinence.
- Be willing and able to provide written informed consent.
- Be at least 21 years of age.

**Key Exclusion Criteria**

- Previous alcoholic liver disease.
- Liver disease caused by hemochromatosis, autoimmune liver disease, Wilson disease, NAFLD, or acute viral hepatitis.
- Sustained alcohol intake below protocol thresholds for longer than the previous 28 days.
- Liver stiffness suggesting fibrosis (FibroScan >7.0 kPa if assessed within the previous 90 days).
- Prior liver transplantation.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.
- Total bilirubin above the upper limit of normal (except Gilbert's syndrome).
- INR >1.4.

*Participants with chronic hepatitis B, chronic hepatitis C, or HIV infection are not excluded.*

**Protocol source:** Section 3, pp. 10–11.

---

### Healthy Controls

**Key Inclusion Criteria**

Participants must:

- Have AUDIT-C scores:
  - Men: <4
  - Women: <3
- Have consumed less than one standard alcoholic drink per week during the previous six months.
- Be willing and able to provide written informed consent.
- Be at least 21 years of age.

**Key Exclusion Criteria**

Participants are excluded for:

- Any history or laboratory evidence of liver disease.
- Diabetes requiring medication.
- Significant heart disease.
- Chronic lung disease requiring treatment.
- Immune-mediated disease.
- HIV infection.
- Recent infection or medications affecting immune function.
- BMI >35.
- Cancer within the previous five years (with protocol-specified exceptions).
- Liver transplantation.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.
- Protocol-defined laboratory abnormalities.
- Investigator judgment that study participation would not be appropriate.

**Protocol source:** Section 3, pp. 11–12.

---

### Eligibility Exceptions / Special Considerations

- Alcoholic hepatitis participants may re-enroll after a new episode occurring at least 24 weeks after the previous enrollment.
- Participants with Gilbert's syndrome may remain eligible under protocol-defined bilirubin criteria.
- Individuals with chronic HBV, HCV, or HIV infection are eligible in specified participant groups as described in the protocol.
- Eligibility exemptions may be granted on a case-by-case basis following review by two independent AlcHepNet investigators using the protocol's exemption process.

**Protocol source:** Section 3 (pp. 10–12); Section 5.2.3 (p. 21).

## Study Timeline and Follow-up

### Index Date / Time Zero

- **Day 0 / index date:** Baseline study visit (enrollment visit after informed consent and eligibility verification).
- **Protocol source (section/page):** Sections 2.2, 5.1, and Table 2 (pp. 8, 16–20).
- **Interpretation for time-based analysis:** All scheduled follow-up visits are measured relative to the baseline visit. For Alcoholic Hepatitis (AH) cases, baseline data are collected prior to initiation of specific AH treatment whenever possible. If treatment has already begun, baseline data are collected as early as possible after hospital admission.

*The baseline visit serves as the anchor for all longitudinal follow-up and biospecimen collection.*

## Statistical Analysis

### Descriptive Analyses

- Subject demographics, anthropometric measurements, medical history, alcohol consumption, laboratory results, MELD score, quality of life, and other collected variables will be summarized by study group (Alcoholic Hepatitis cases, Heavy Drinking Controls, and Healthy Controls) and by study visit.
- Continuous variables will be summarized using means and 95% confidence intervals.
- Categorical variables will be summarized using frequencies and proportions.

**Protocol source:** Section 10 (pp. 23–24).

---

### Group Comparisons

- Two-sample t-tests will be used to compare continuous variables between study groups at each visit.
- Chi-square tests will be used to compare categorical variables between study groups.
- When assumptions of normality are violated, appropriate nonparametric tests will be used.

**Protocol source:** Section 10 (pp. 23–24).

---

### Longitudinal / Time-to-Event Analyses

The observational cohort is intended to support future ancillary studies requiring longitudinal analyses.

Depending on the specific research question, proposed analyses may include:

- Cox proportional hazards regression for time-to-event outcomes.
- Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) for repeated measurements.
- Generalized linear mixed-effects models for longitudinal outcomes.
- Linear, logistic, or Poisson regression models as appropriate for individual ancillary study proposals.

These analyses are described as potential methods for future approved studies rather than analyses for the primary observational protocol itself.

**Protocol source:** Section 10 (pp. 23–24).

---

### Missing Data

The protocol does **not** specify a single predefined missing-data strategy for the observational cohort.

Instead:

- Future ancillary study proposals must specify the expected amount of missing data.
- Each proposal must define the statistical methods that will be used to address missingness.

**Protocol source:** Section 10 (pp. 23–24).

---

### Statistical Software

- SAS version 9.3 (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina)

**Protocol source:** Section 10 (p. 23).

---

### Additional / Ancillary Analyses

The observational cohort serves as a resource for future consortium studies.

Future ancillary studies must include:

- Clearly defined primary and secondary outcomes.
- Sample size calculations.
- Type I and Type II error assumptions.
- Minimum clinically important differences.
- Statistical power of at least 0.80.
- Study-specific statistical analysis plans.

These proposals are reviewed for scientific merit and feasibility before approval.

**Protocol source:** Section 10 (pp. 23–24).

## Pitfalls

- Healthy controls participate only at baseline, resulting in limited longitudinal comparison with the other study groups.
- Several biospecimens (PBMC, liver tissue, stool, urine, saliva) are collected only when available or only at selected sites, which may affect completeness for downstream analyses.
- Because this is an observational cohort, treatment decisions are determined by standard clinical care rather than by protocol, introducing potential confounding for future comparative analyses.
