# AlcHepNet Biorepository Knowledge Base

## Purpose

The AlcHepNet Biorepository & Laboratory Manual establishes standardized laboratory procedures for biospecimen collection, processing, labeling, storage, shipment, inventory management, quality control, and specimen distribution across all participating AlcHepNet clinical sites.

Unlike the clinical study protocols, which define participant eligibility, interventions, and study endpoints, this manual governs laboratory operations and biospecimen management.

Its primary objective is to ensure that specimens collected across multiple institutions are processed consistently so they remain suitable for current analyses and future translational research.

---

# Scope

The manual covers the complete biospecimen lifecycle:

1. specimen collection
2. laboratory processing
3. aliquoting
4. barcode labeling
5. temporary storage
6. shipment to the UMMS Biorepository
7. shipment receipt
8. inventory management
9. long-term storage
10. specimen release for approved research

Clinical care, participant eligibility, treatment assignment, and study outcomes are governed by the study protocols rather than this manual.

# Biospecimen Collection and Processing

This section summarizes the standardized laboratory procedures used throughout AlcHepNet for collecting, processing, aliquoting, labeling, and freezing biospecimens.

Although each specimen type has its own SOP, the overall workflow is standardized to minimize pre-analytical variability between participating sites.

---

# General Collection Principles

All participating sites should follow the same general collection principles.

- Collect specimens according to the protocol-specific visit schedule.
- Use protocol-approved collection tubes, cryovials, and laboratory supplies (or approved equivalents).
- Process specimens within the required processing windows.
- Apply UMMS barcode labels before freezing.
- Record all required collection and processing information.
- Maintain specimen identity throughout handling.
- Document protocol deviations.
- Follow institutional biosafety procedures.

Study-specific specimen schedules are provided in the protocol appendices (Observational Study, Randomized Clinical Trial, and sample-handling appendices).

# Platelet-Poor Plasma (PPP)

## Purpose

Platelet-poor plasma is collected for future biomarker, cytokine, proteomic, metabolomic, and other translational analyses.

The objective is to obtain plasma with minimal platelet contamination using a standardized double-centrifugation protocol.

---

## Collection

- Three citrate tubes are processed for platelet-poor plasma.
- A fourth citrate tube is reserved for acidified plasma (when applicable).

Blood should be mixed immediately after collection by gently inverting the tubes 8–10 times.

---

## Critical Parameters

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Processing window | Immediately when possible; within 2 hours if delayed |
| First centrifugation | 1500 × g for 15 minutes |
| Second centrifugation | 1500 × g for 15 minutes |
| Temperature | Room temperature |
| Storage | −80°C |

---

## Workflow

1. Collect citrate blood.
2. Mix by gentle inversion.
3. Perform first centrifugation.
4. Carefully transfer plasma without disturbing the buffy coat.
5. Perform second centrifugation.
6. Leave approximately 100 μL above the pellet.
7. Prepare six aliquots (approximately 1 mL each).
8. Apply barcode labels.
9. Freeze immediately.

---

## Expected Yield

- Approximately six aliquots
- Approximately 1 mL per aliquot
- Light blue-cap cryovials

---

## Critical Quality Points

- Completely fill citrate tubes.
- Avoid disturbing the buffy coat.
- Process promptly.
- Freeze immediately after aliquoting.
- Verify barcode labels before storage.

---

# Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cells (PBMC)

## Purpose

PBMCs are preserved for future immunologic, genomic, mechanistic, and translational research.

Because PBMC viability depends heavily on standardized handling, this is the most technically demanding procedure in the MOP.

---

## Collection

- Three 8 mL CPT sodium citrate tubes
- Approximately 24 mL total blood

---

## Materials

Major materials include:

- CPT tubes
- PBS
- Fetal bovine serum (FBS)
- DMSO
- Controlled-rate freezing container
- Orange-cap cryovials

---

## Critical Parameters

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Begin processing | Within 12 hours of collection |
| Primary centrifugation | 1800 × g |
| Duration | 30 minutes |
| Temperature | Room temperature |
| Brake | OFF |
| Wash centrifugation | 300 × g for 10 minutes |
| Storage | Controlled-rate freezing followed by −80°C (or liquid nitrogen when available) |

---

## Workflow

1. Record collection time.
2. Keep CPT tubes upright at room temperature.
3. Process within 12 hours.
4. Centrifuge using standardized CPT conditions.
5. Remove CPT plasma.
6. Collect PBMC layer.
7. Perform PBS wash steps.
8. Count viable cells.
9. Suspend cells in DMSO/FBS freezing medium.
10. Aliquot into cryovials.
11. Perform controlled-rate freezing.
12. Transfer to long-term storage.

---

## Expected Yield

- Approximately eleven PBMC aliquots
- Approximately 1–2 million viable cells per aliquot

---

## Critical Quality Points

- Do not exceed the processing window.
- Never use the centrifuge brake during primary separation.
- Avoid disturbing the mononuclear cell layer.
- Combine no more than three CPT tubes per processing tube.
- Record viable cell counts.
- Use controlled-rate freezing for every specimen.

---

# Serum

## Purpose

Serum is collected for future biomarker and laboratory analyses.

---

## Collection

- Red-top serum tube
- Allow blood to clot before centrifugation

---

## Processing

- Centrifuge after clot formation.
- Transfer serum without disturbing the clot.
- Prepare six aliquots.
- Freeze immediately.

---

## Expected Yield

- Six aliquots
- Approximately 0.75 mL each
- Red-cap cryovials

---

# Anakinra Trough Serum

Collected only for participants receiving anakinra in the randomized clinical trial.

Used for pharmacokinetic assessment.

Processing follows the standard serum procedure with dedicated specimen labeling.

---

# Whole Blood (DNA)

Whole blood is preserved for future genomic analyses.

General workflow:

- collect in green-top tube
- aliquot whole blood
- freeze without centrifugation

Expected yield:

- one whole-blood aliquot

---

# Red Blood Cell Lysates

Prepared after whole blood processing.

General workflow:

- remove plasma
- prepare RBC lysate
- prepare three aliquots
- freeze immediately

---

# Acidified Plasma

Collected only for participants specified in the MOP.

Processing follows the platelet-poor plasma procedure with hydrochloric acid added before aliquoting.

---

# Platelet-Rich Plasma

Prepared using centrifugation conditions that intentionally retain platelets.

Typical preparation:

- six aliquots
- approximately 0.5 mL each
- lavender-cap cryovials
- −80°C storage

---

# Urine

Purpose:

Biomarker and metabolomic analyses.

General workflow:

- collect approximately 40 mL
- mix thoroughly
- prepare seven aliquots
- freeze immediately

---

# Stool

Purpose:

Microbiome and gastrointestinal research.

Important handling requirements:

- transport on ice whenever possible
- minimize room-temperature exposure
- prepare four aliquots
- freeze promptly

---

# Saliva

Collected using the OMNIgene saliva collection kit.

Provides DNA for future molecular analyses.

---

# Liver Biopsy Tissue

Collected according to study-specific protocol procedures.

Handled and stored using standardized procedures to preserve tissue quality for future histologic and translational studies.

# Specimen Labeling and Identification

Accurate specimen identification is fundamental to maintaining chain of custody throughout the AlcHepNet Biorepository. Every research aliquot receives a standardized barcode label generated by the UMMS Biorepository before specimen collection.

The labeling system allows every aliquot to be uniquely identified from collection through long-term storage and eventual research distribution.

Clinical laboratory tubes continue to follow each site's institutional labeling procedures. The AlcHepNet barcode system applies to research cryovials stored within the biorepository.

---

## Barcode Label Structure

Each barcode uniquely identifies a specimen using standardized components.

| Component | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Subject ID | Participant study identifier |
| Study Identifier | Randomized Clinical Trial or Observational Study |
| Collection Time Point | Study day or study week |
| Specimen Type | Two-character specimen code |
| Aliquot Number | Sequential aliquot identifier |

Example:

```
99009R007SE01
```

This indicates:

- Subject 99009
- Randomized Clinical Trial
- Day 7 collection
- Serum specimen
- Aliquot 01

# Storage

Following processing, specimens remain frozen at the participating clinical site until scheduled shipment.

General storage principles include:

- freeze specimens immediately after processing whenever required
- maintain protocol-defined temperatures
- minimize freeze–thaw cycles
- maintain accurate freezer inventories
- restrict specimen access to authorized personnel

Most specimens are maintained at **−80°C**.

PBMC specimens undergo controlled-rate freezing before long-term storage and may subsequently be transferred to liquid nitrogen storage when available.

---
