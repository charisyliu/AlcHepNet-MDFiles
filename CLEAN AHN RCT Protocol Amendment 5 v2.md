# AlcHepNet-02 Randomized Clinical Trial

## Summary

This study is a **Phase 2B multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial** evaluating whether **Anakinra plus zinc** improves outcomes compared with **prednisone** in hospitalized adults with **severe alcoholic hepatitis (AH)**.

The rationale is based on the limited efficacy and infection risk associated with corticosteroids. Prednisone remains standard therapy but offers only modest short-term survival benefit. Anakinra inhibits the interleukin-1 (IL-1) inflammatory pathway, while zinc is intended to improve intestinal barrier function and reduce endotoxin-mediated inflammation.

Approximately **258 participants** are randomized in a **1:1 ratio** to one of two treatment strategies and followed for **180 days**.

The principal efficacy endpoint is **90-day survival**, while secondary analyses examine organ dysfunction, infection, renal outcomes, transplant-free survival, biomarkers, and development of improved endpoint definitions for future alcoholic hepatitis trials.

**Protocol source:** Executive Summary; Sections 2–4.

# Study Objectives

## Primary Objective

- Determine whether **Anakinra plus zinc is superior to prednisone** for treatment of severe alcoholic hepatitis.
- Compare clinical efficacy and safety of the two treatment strategies.
- Evaluate whether inhibition of IL-1–mediated inflammation improves survival.

# Eligibility Criteria

## Key Inclusion Criteria

Participants must:

- Be at least 21 years old.
- Have severe alcoholic hepatitis.
- Meet protocol diagnostic criteria.
- Have MELD score between 20 and 35 on Day 0.

Major exclusions include:

- Alternative causes of liver disease explaining presentation
- Contraindication to study medications
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding
- Active malignancy meeting exclusion criteria
- Severe uncontrolled infection requiring exclusion
- Prior liver transplantation
- Other protocol-defined medical conditions that could interfere with study participation or interpretation of results

---

## Dose Modification

The protocol uses **fixed-dose therapy** rather than individualized dose escalation.

General principles include:

- No routine dose titration.
- Dose interruption is permitted when participant safety requires temporary cessation.
- Permanent discontinuation may occur if continued treatment presents unacceptable risk.
- Investigators document all dose interruptions and the reason for modification.
- Participants remain in study follow-up whenever possible after discontinuing study medication.

---

## Temporary Treatment Holding

Study medication may be temporarily withheld for clinically important events, including:

- Serious infection requiring investigator assessment.
- Significant allergic or hypersensitivity reactions.
- Development of unexpected severe toxicity.
- Participant inability to safely receive study medication.
- Investigator judgment that temporary interruption is medically necessary.

Temporary interruption does **not** automatically withdraw the participant from study follow-up.

---

## Permanent Treatment Discontinuation

Study medication may be permanently discontinued for reasons including:

- Participant withdrawal of consent.
- Investigator determination that continued treatment is unsafe.
- Development of exclusionary medical conditions after enrollment.
- Severe adverse reaction attributable to study medication.
- Pregnancy.
- Protocol violation requiring discontinuation.
- Death.
- Liver transplantation.
- Sponsor or DSMB recommendation.

Whenever feasible, participants who discontinue treatment continue protocol-defined outcome assessments through Day 180.

---

## Early Study Withdrawal

Participants may withdraw from study participation at any time.

Investigators attempt to obtain:

- Reason for withdrawal
- Survival status
- Major clinical outcomes
- Final safety assessment
- Serious adverse event follow-up (when applicable)

Every effort is made to minimize missing endpoint data.

# Statistical Analysis

## Analysis Populations

### Intention-to-Treat (ITT)

The primary efficacy analysis will be performed using the **Intention-to-Treat (ITT)** population.

The ITT population includes **all randomized participants**, analyzed according to their assigned treatment group regardless of:

- Treatment adherence
- Treatment discontinuation
- Protocol deviations
- Early withdrawal from study medication

This preserves the benefits of randomization and reflects real-world treatment effectiveness.

---

### Per-Protocol (PP)

Per-protocol analyses may be conducted as supportive analyses.

The PP population includes participants who:

- Meet all major eligibility criteria,
- Receive protocol-defined study treatment,
- Have no major protocol violations that would substantially affect efficacy assessment.

PP analyses are considered secondary to the ITT analysis.

---

### Safety Population

The Safety Population consists of **all participants who receive at least one dose of study medication**.

Safety analyses include:

- Adverse events (AEs)
- Serious adverse events (SAEs)
- Laboratory abnormalities
- Treatment discontinuation due to toxicity
- Infection-related outcomes
- Mortality during follow-up

# Quick Reference (2-Minute Summary)

**Disease:** Severe alcoholic hepatitis.

**Study Design:** Phase 2B, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.

**Population:** Approximately 258 hospitalized adults with severe AH (MELD 20–35).

**Treatment Arms:**

- Anakinra (100 mg SC Days 1–14) + Zinc sulfate (220 mg PO Days 1–90)
- Prednisone (40 mg PO Days 1–30) with matching placebos

**Primary Endpoint:** 90-day all-cause survival.

**Follow-up:** Through Day 180.

**Major Clinical Scores:**

- MELD
- Lille
- SOFA
- Modified SOFA
- qSOFA

**Major Safety Outcomes:**

- Infection
- Sepsis
- AKI
- Multi-organ failure
- Mortality

**Primary Statistical Methods:**

- Kaplan–Meier survival analysis
- Log-rank test
- Cox proportional hazards model

**Data Capture:** REDCap.

**Regulatory Oversight:** FDA IND 144031, WIRB, independent DSMB.
